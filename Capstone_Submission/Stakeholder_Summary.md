# Summary of Findings & Recommendations
*Week 4 Capstone — Sales analysis of 700 orders, Sep 2013 – Dec 2014*

**Headline:** Net sales of **$118.7M** earned **$16.9M profit (14.2% margin)**. Profit is concentrated in one segment, and one segment loses money.

## Key findings
- **Government is the engine:** 44% of sales and 67% of profit. Government and Small Business together are about 80% of sales.
- **Enterprise loses money:** a −3.1% margin (−$0.61M profit). All 58 loss-making orders (−$0.78M) are Enterprise orders, and its cost of goods is about 103% of net sales.
- **Discounts erode profit:** margin is 21.9% with no discount, 17.9% low, 14.4% medium and 9.1% high. The High and Medium bands already carry 64% of sales.
- **Geography is not the issue:** each country sells $21M–$25M.
- **Seasonality:** October and December are the peak months. Because 2013 only covers Sep–Dec, the like-for-like (Sep–Dec) growth is about 37%, not the 3.5x seen in raw totals.
- **Predicting sales:** price, volume and discount band predict sales almost exactly (R² = 0.997). Without volume the model explains only about 55–62%, so the volume forecast is the key unknown.

## Recommendations
1. **Fix Enterprise economics** — raise price, cut unit cost or limit discounts so no order is sold below cost (break-even alone is worth about $0.6M).
2. **Govern discounting** — require approval above the Medium band; steer High-band deals toward Low.
3. **Protect Government, but diversify** — it supplies two-thirds of profit.
4. **Test scaling Channel Partners and Midmarket** — 73% and 28% margins on small bases; confirm margins hold at volume.
5. **Plan for the Oct/Dec peaks** in inventory and promotions.
6. **Forecast units** by segment and month and apply the price × discount model.

*Caveat: 16 months and 700 orders; results describe this dataset only.*
